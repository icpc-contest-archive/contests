## The headline pattern

Your rules produce a much cleaner sort than my tier table did, and two of them turn out
to be nearly the same rule in practice: **almost every online event in the pile is also an
identity-less preliminary of an already-catalogued parent** (Japan's First-Round, Korea's
First Round, Taiwan's TOPC, Indonesia's INC, Tehran's Internet round, the Dhaka/Amritapuri
prelims, China's EC online rounds, the NERC quals). So adopting R4 as *"not an
open-internet round; distributed-but-proctored university sites count as in-person;
force-majeure years excepted"* costs you almost nothing that the identity lens wouldn't
already exclude — and it never has to fire against a real contest.

The cases that actually needed research came out like this:

1. **Ukraine** — better than expected, with a twist: the six region rows per year are
   per-site rankings of ONE simultaneous nationwide round (common server, one problemset —
   2025 regulations say so explicitly), onsite through 2019, online since 2020 for
   COVID-then-war reasons (the force-majeure case). It gates Stage III → SEERC with
   published quotas. Mainline — but as *one merged contest per year* carrying the regional
   rankings, not six sibling contests.
2. **Two of my assumed "feeders" are your KTH case exactly**: **MIUP** (Portugal) and
   **Ada Byron** (Spain) are real, onsite, multi-university, fresh-problem national
   contests that gate *nothing* — SWERC entry is open per-university registration, and Ada
   Byron's national final even runs in July, after the SWERC season. Registry, both.
3. **Moldova** — correction to the Putka-analogy hypothesis in your own notes: unlike
   Slovenia, there is no evidence anything ever ran. Only the school-level republican
   olympiad exists; SEERC needs no national stage (direct university registration). The 11
   empty CMS shells look like annual provisioning that was never used. Nothing to catalogue.
4. **GP Mexico is not an online series** — the sede manual mandates teams sit physically
   at registered university sites ("Presencial distribuido"); my flight-pack worry was wrong.
5. **Bangladesh NCPC is a standalone national championship** — the Dhaka regional has its
   own open preliminary, and NCPC placement gates nothing. A real contest that fails R3.
6. The genuinely hard calls are three: **Vietnam National** (real identity, gates the
   regional in writing, but delivered on an online judge at proctored multi-site venues),
   **Kazakhstan Octafinal** (onsite + own identity, but it is tier-labeled "1/8 финала"
   like the NERC quals we'd exclude), and **Brazil First Phase** (passes everything; only
   its *name* says "phase of" — 42–54 host sites, ~1,000 teams, own SBC set, published
   advancement rules).

## Cards for the contested calls

### Ukraine — mainline, as ONE merged contest per year (high)

**Facts.** These rows are Stage II ("1/4 першості світу") of the All-Ukrainian
championship: run *simultaneously at all base universities on a common server with one
problemset* (2025 regulations, verbatim), regions hosted at LNU Lviv (W), NURE Kharkiv (E),
NUBiP Kyiv (N), ONAFT Odesa (S), VNTU Vinnytsia (C), Kyiv-city some years, a Southwestern
region in 2018. ~40 onsite teams per region, 20+ institutions each; ~123 median registered.
Chain: Stage I (oblast) → Stage II → Stage III final → SEERC, with published quotas — and
the quotas are computed on the **unified** standings (per-oblast, per-institution,
national top-100), not per region. Onsite through 2019; online 2020 (COVID) and 2022+
(war), codified in the 2025 regulations — your force-majeure exception, exactly.
**For:** R1 ✓ (multi-university per region), R2 ✓ (shared-sibling clause, and advancement
depends on geography), R3 ✓ (explicit quota chain to SEERC), R4 ✓ (online only under
force majeure). **Against:** the regions have no jury/problemset/identity of their own —
they are venue partitions of one round; region composition is unstable year to year.
**Proposal:** mainline as `ukraine-stage2-YYYY` (or similar), ONE edition per year with
the per-region rankings attached — not six sibling contests. ~15 merged editions instead
of 81 rows.

### Brazil First Phase — mainline (med-high)

**Facts.** Same-day onsite at 42–54 university sedes (556–1,025 teams, 170–239 schools);
own SBC-authored problemset (statement PDFs titled "Sub-Regional Brasil do ICPC"); online
only 2020–21. ~65 teams advance by published vaga rules to the Fase Nacional (our
`brazil`), Brazil's ranking of the joint LatAm regional. **For:** R1/R2/R3/R4 all pass;
it is the biggest onsite contest in the hemisphere. **Against:** the identity lens — it
is *named* the first phase of its parent and spans the same geography. But unlike a NERC
qual it has its own set, its own day, sedes, and a 17-year public record. **Proposal:**
mainline; the strict-lens alternative (fold into `brazil` as a qualifying-round note) is
defensible but loses 1,000-team results.

### Vietnam National — mainline, R4-borderline (med)

**Facts.** Gates the Asia Vietnam regional *in writing* (≥1 problem solved required for
selectability), runs ~1 month before it; organized by VAIP; delivery is an online judge
(Kattis 2018–19, VNOJ 2024) but teams sit at proctored multi-site "centralized
locations". **For:** R1/R2/R3 ✓, real identity, real gate, correct ordering.
**Against:** if "online judge at proctored sites" counts as online, R4 excludes it — but
by that reading Brazil's distributed sedes and GP Mexico's fechas are online too.
**Proposal:** mainline, and define R4 as venue-based (proctored university sites =
in-person), not platform-based.

### Kazakhstan Octafinal vs Moscow vs the NERC quals — one policy, three instances

**Facts.** NERC's vocabulary: qual = "1/8 финала", subregional = "четвертьфинал", NERC =
semifinal. The quals are online by design (NWRRC's was online in 2019, before COVID, and
still is), have no venue or name of their own → part-of-parent. Moscow's qualification
had a brand ("Moscow Programming Contest" 2018–19) but teams sit at home universities and
the brand was absorbed into "MRC — отборочный этап"; online 2020–22 → part-of-parent.
Kazakhstan's Octafinal (2024+) is the outlier: **onsite** ("в очном форматe, в стенах
ВУЗа"), own name, own ICPC finder listing and standings, gating the Kostanay/Almaty
quarterfinal. **Proposal:** part-of-parent for the quals and Moscow; mainline for the
Octafinal *if you accept distributed-onsite + it recurring* — otherwise file it with the
quals for consistency. Your call is really about whether own-identity + onsite promotes a
tier-labeled round.

### Oman sub-regions — mainline except Seniors (med-high)

**Facts.** New structure, onsite: 2025 = three qualifiers (Muscat/Capital, Sohar/Coastline,
Nizwa/Midland), 2026 = five (+ Dhofar/Southern, Sharqiyah/Oriental; finder row
OCPCCap-2026 exists); 21 institutions, 65 teams nationally; MTCIT + SQU + UTAS organize;
gates the OCPC final at SQU → ACPC → WF. **For:** R1 ✓ (several governorates'
institutions each), R3 ✓ (documented chain), R4 ✓ (onsite). **Against:** R2 unverified —
whether the simultaneous sub-regions share one set (Ukraine-style) is unknown; two
seasons of history only. The **Seniors** row has no evidence and no WF path → registry.
**Proposal:** mainline (your lean holds), with the sharing question flagged for when
results land.

### Caribbean CNC / CFQ — one series, one wrinkle (high)

**Facts.** 2010–2019: per-country simultaneous onsite nationals on one shared Caribbean
set, gating the Caribbean Finals (itself a ranking inside the synchronized LatAm
regional). 2020+: replaced by the **CFQ/Eliminatoria — one online contest** on Matcom
Online Grader (born in COVID, still online by design; 2026 edition Oct 3), per-country
quotas unchanged. **Proposal:** CNC years mainline (they pass everything); for 2020+
either treat CFQ as the same series' online era (continuity, with format notes) or
registry it under strict R4. I lean continuity: same function, same organizer lineage,
COVID-born.

### Bolivia — mainline with a bald R2 caveat (med)

**Facts.** Bolivia's qualifier has *never had its own problemset*: it is the Bolivian
ranking of the September SBC-set contest (116→227 teams), gating the SA-South Tarija
site; a separate Bolivian-authored "Pre-National" CF gym precedes it. **For:** R1/R3/R4 ✓.
**Against:** R2 only via the shared-set clause — but the sharing partner is another
country's national phase, not a sibling site of the same contest. If you accept "the
September SBC block is one giant multi-country contest with per-country gates", Bolivia
is fine; if R2 requires an own set at least sometimes, Bolivia is a ranking, not a
contest. **Proposal:** mainline (the qualification-depends-on-the-split logic you already
endorsed for SA), noting it is the extreme case of it.

### MIUP and Ada Byron — your KTH case, twice (med/high)

If you ever want a "notable national championships" shelf outside mainline, these are
its charter members alongside KTH's contest.

## The problemset-sharing matrix (your "really a single contest" question)

Two distinct layers in LatAm, and you were right about one of them:

**Regional layer (November):** since 2009 *every* LatAm+Caribbean regional runs
same-day on ONE synchronized problemset (LAR rules: "synchronized… same problem set") —
SA-North, SA-South, Brazil's Fase Nacional, Mexico's Final, the CA sites and the
Caribbean Finals are per-subregion rankings of one contest; "Venezuela Finals" and the
2022 "Ecuador Finals" are per-country cuts of it. Qualification depends entirely on the
split, so split entries stand — but the catalogue could carry a `shared_set_with:` note.

**National layer (Aug–Oct):** the Brazilian SBC First-Phase set is a shared September
block: 2021 = BR + GP-MX 3ª fecha + GP-CA 3ª + TAP; 2022 = + Bolivia (TAP's last shared
year); 2023 = BR + MX + CA + Bolivia + Torneo Chileno (Argentina out); 2024–26 = BR + MX
3ª + CA + Bolivia. Bolivia's whole qualifier IS the shared contest; Mexico/CA share one
fecha of several; TAP and TCP were literally one joint event in 2019; GP MX-y-CA was one
event 2015–17. **Colombia never shares** — own problems, own date, every year.

Elsewhere: Ukraine's regions = one set, many sites (see card); Caribbean CNC countries =
one Caribbean set; the NERC quals do NOT share (independent juries and dates — genuinely
distinct rounds, unlike Ukraine).
